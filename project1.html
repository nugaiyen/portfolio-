Spatial Distribution and Reliability of the Population Aged 65+, King County By Lindsay Nguyen 01.26.2026 Understanding where older adults live is essential for planning healthcare access, emergency response, housing, and social services. This analysis maps the estimated population aged 65 and older across King County, Washington, using census tract level data. Because these estimates are derived from sample data, this project also incorporates measures of uncertainty to ensure that spatial patterns are interpreted appropriately and responsibly. The American Community Survey (ACS) provides small area demographic data, but as a sample based estimate, it can contain sampling error that varies across census tracts, especially in areas with smaller populations. To address this, the coefficient of variation (CV) is mapped alongside the population aged 65 and older, with higher CVs indicating greater uncertainty. This analysis uses CDC/ATSDR Social Vulnerability Index (SVI) 2022 data, based on the 2017–2021 ACS five-year estimates, allowing for more cautious interpretation of older adult populations across King County. Adults Aged 65+ Figure 1 shows that the estimated number of residents aged 65 and older varies considerably across King County. The highest counts are concentrated in densely populated census tracts, particularly near Seattle and along major waterfront areas, as well as east of I-90 beginning around Issaquah, where overall population levels are higher. Vashon Island also shows a noticeably high number of older residents. These patterns likely reflect the presence of larger, well-established neighborhoods that support aging in place. In terms of reliability, many urban census tracts exhibit relatively low coefficients of variation (CVs), indicating higher confidence in the estimated counts. However, a substantial portion of the county displays medium CV values, suggesting moderate reliability. These tracts are primarily located in eastern and southern King County, extending from areas east and south of Duvall through Enumclaw. Several tracts with high estimated counts of adults aged 65 and older, particularly in less densely populated areas, also show higher CVs, indicating greater uncertainty in these estimates. This highlights the importance of examining population counts alongside their CVs, as high estimates do not necessarily imply high reliability. Tracts with lower CV values are more commonly found near major transportation corridors, especially within approximately 10 miles of I-5 and I-405, reflecting the greater stability of estimates in larger urban populations. While there is no single, consistent spatial pattern for low CVs, they are frequently observed in western cities such as Seattle, Redmond, Kent, Bellevue, and Renton. Vashon Island shows a mix of low and medium CVs, indicating that the reliability of estimates varies across the island. Percent Adults Aged 65+ Figure 2 shows the percentage of people aged 65 and older across the county. Higher percentages are concentrated mainly in the eastern and southeastern areas, south of I-90 and north of the Alpine Lakes Wilderness. Vashon Island, Richmond Beach in Shoreline, and Sammamish also show relatively high percentages of older adults. These areas are generally more suburban or rural, where older adults make up a larger share of the population. In contrast, central Seattle and other densely populated urban areas have lower percentages of older residents, likely reflecting a younger population associated with employment centers. Similarly to Figure 1, Vashon Island stands out, with several census tracts falling into the highest percentage categories, indicating an older population overall. Parts of eastern King County also has high percentages of older adults despite having lower total population counts. One possible explanation is that while urban areas may contain large numbers of older residents, they often represent a smaller proportion of the total population. The bottom map in Figure 2 displays the coefficients of variation (CVs) for the percentage estimates. Lower CVs are concentrated in western King County, particularly around Seattle and nearby cities, likely reflecting greater reliability in areas with larger populations. Eastern and southern King County mostly exhibit medium CVs. Vashon Island shows low CV values, indicating a relatively high level of confidence in the estimates. Compared to the CV map in Figure 1, Figure 2 includes more areas with low CVs, such as locations near the Green River and Sammamish. Overall, western King County, especially along coastal and waterfront areas, tends to have lower CV values than the rest of the county, and this pattern is more pronounced than in Figure 1. Conclusion This analysis of the population aged 65 and older in King County highlights the importance of examining both population counts and percentage-based measures alongside their associated coefficients of variation. Results indicate that urban and suburban areas in western King County, such as Seattle, Bellevue, Redmond, Kent, Renton, and Shoreline, contain the largest numbers of older adults, though these residents often represent a smaller proportion of the total population. In contrast, less densely populated areas, including Vashon Island, Sammamish, Issaquah, and parts of eastern and southeastern King County near Duvall and Enumclaw, show higher percentages of older adults despite lower overall population counts. The CV maps further reveal spatial differences in data reliability across the county. Census tracts in Seattle and surrounding western cities generally display lower CV values, indicating greater confidence in the ACS estimates, while tracts in eastern and southern King County more frequently exhibit medium CVs, has increased uncertainty. Notably, Vashon Island shows consistently high percentages of older adults with relatively low CV values, suggesting that these patterns are both meaningful and reliable. Overall, this analysis shows that, whether considering population counts or percentages of adults aged 65 and older, a wide range of values can still correspond to medium coefficients of variation. In other words, high or low estimates do not necessarily guarantee greater reliability. At the same time, areas with larger populations generally have lower CVs, indicating more reliable data. These patterns highlight the importance of evaluating both the magnitude of the data and its associated uncertainty when making planning and policy decisions.
